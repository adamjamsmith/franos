
# Franchise Story Builder

Builds an episode of **Franchise Stories** (Adam Smith's Starter Story-style YouTube channel about franchisees) from a franchisee's real interview. Output: a beat sheet showing the best possible version of their story, plus a re-interview question list designed to get them to say it in their own words.

v1, built from one Starter Story teardown (David Park, 2.2M views) and Episode 1 (David Sandelands, Enviro Clean + Oven Rescue). Reference docs in the Franchise Stories project: `teardowns/01-david-park-starter-story.md`, `episodes/ep01-david-sandelands-beat-sheet.md`. Update this playbook as more teardowns are done.

## Non-negotiables

- **Never invent facts.** Mark every line ✅ (they said it, with transcript timestamp) or ❓ (our read between the lines, to confirm in the re-interview). Assumptions are allowed in the draft only if marked ❓, and every ❓ becomes a question.
- **Work from the uncut transcript**, not from summaries or ad cuts. Ask Adam for the folder if you don't have it (usually `Documents\websites\<Brand>\Testimonials\<Name>`).
- **Check the client's do-not-say rules** (the brand's Brain Dump, section "Things we must not say"): competitor names, sister brands, dark detail, unverified claims. Flag conflicts and ask for the franchisor's sign-off rather than silently dropping the story.
- **Numbers:** use only figures the franchisee and franchisor will stand behind (they act as earnings claims). List conflicting figures in a "check before publishing" section.
- **Adam's style when he reviews:** tight bullet fragments, not prose. One idea per bullet. No over-explaining.

## The theme (every episode)

**"Back yourself."** Tagline after the thesis: *"I'm Adam Smith, and this is Franchise Stories. Back yourself."*

- They already had the cards. They were playing them for someone else.
- Identity follows action: you find out who you are by acting.
- **The franchise is the certainty. The uncertainty is inside them.** The model is proven; the battle is "could *I* do it?" Every ⬇ beat is a self-doubt that grows with the stakes. Every ⬆ is the proven system plus a skill they already had.
- Franchising doesn't make people braver. It makes the first action small enough to take, and lets them find out what they're capable of without betting everything alone.
- Weave this in. Never say "they had the skills all along" out loud.

## Story mechanics

- **Rags to riches, inner change.** The money is the proof; the change in the person is the story. Want vs need. The ending mirrors the beginning (e.g. made redundant three times → now three people's jobs depend on him).
- **Find the spine:** the deep reason (usually family security, time, control) set up in Act 1, threatened in Act 2, paid off at the end.
- **But / therefore, never "and then".** Each beat is caused by the last or complicates it. A lucky break (the find) must follow from desperation, so they were ready to see it.
- **Ups and downs alternate.** Lows get deeper as the stakes rise. Don't make it look easy: "simple, but you have to graft."
- **Set up and pay off skills silently.** Show a skill from their old job early (selling, training, managing) and let it pay off later without comment. Put setup/payoff notes in italics for the edit only.
- **Narrator = plot, franchisee = feeling.** VO states facts, stakes and turns in short lines. The franchisee gives scenes and emotion.
- **Scenes beat summaries.** Specific moments (the phone call, the kitchen table, the first job) are what people share.
- **A number at every beat** (the scoreboard).
- **Name the universal moment** when there is one, e.g. VO: "Every franchisee hits this point. The van is full. Do you keep working it, or do you scale?"
- **Lesson for the viewer** in the middle (their playbook: ad spend, first hire, jobs a day).
- **The mentor is the franchisor** (e.g. Charlie and Mark). Show them doing things at each bet; don't just mention them.
- **Ending:** a final test that answers the viewer's last doubt ("was it a fluke?"), a callback to the opening, a contrast (start vs now), what it means for their future, an open loop (what's next), then end on their single best line and cut to black. VO bridge into the CTA (e.g. "David found it at 51. When will you?").
- **CTAs:** prospectus at ~25% (straight after the find) and at the end.

## Beat sheet structure

Adapt beat names and count to the story; keep the acts and functions.

**ACT 1: UNDER THE THUMB** (~45%)
1. **Cold open:** the lows (lifted from anywhere in the interview), the intriguing turn, the thesis: "This is the story of how [name] went from [low] to [outcome] in [time]." + tagline.
2. ⬇ **Ordinary world:** who they were told to be; working hard for someone else's outcome; skills set up silently; what it cost the family.
3. ⬇⬇ **Final straw:** the moment they can't live with the uncertainty.
4. ⬆ **The find / the sign:** how they found the brand; their homework; the model works. **CTA #1.**
5. ⬇ **The doubt:** the outside question is answered, the inside one isn't: "could I do it?"
6. ⬆ **The bet:** their decision line.

**ACT 2: SOWING** (~35%)
7. ⬇ **The grind:** the uncertainty of it all resting on them (not just the work).
8. ⬆ **The seed:** old skills come in handy; it's not rocket science.
9. ⬆ **The harvest:** repeat work, referrals, diary fills. "You reap what you sow" works as VO.
10. ⬇ **The crossroads:** capacity reached; keep working it or scale?
11. ⬆ **The choice:** the scary step (first hire, second van); it turns out to be something they already knew how to do.

**ACT 3: THE LEVEL UP / TODAY** (~20%)
- Proven → "why can't I go further?" → the bigger bet → final test → callback → contrast → what it means → open loop → last line → VO → **CTA #2.**

If the franchisee's story is simpler (one business, no scaling), compress Act 3 to: now, contrast, what it means, last line.

## Process

1. **Gather:** the uncut transcript, the brand's Brain Dump (figures, do-not-say rules, avatar), any existing cuts.
2. **Extract the real beats:** list what actually happened in order with timestamps. Note corrections to anything previously assumed.
3. **Find the theme fit:** their wound, old belief, want, need, the skills they brought, the spine, the mirror ending.
4. **Draft the best possible story** in the structure above, ✅/❓ on every line, ⬆/⬇ on every beat, but/therefore links checked.
5. **Review with Adam** in tight bullets. Expect several rounds; he adds the human read between the lines. Keep his additions, marked ❓.
6. **Write the re-interview questions,** grouped by beat (see below).
7. **Add "check before publishing":** franchisor sign-offs, conflicting figures, claims needing proof, competitor mentions to cut, age/date consistency.
8. **Save** to the Franchise Stories project as `episodes/epNN-<name>-beat-sheet.md`.

## Re-interview questions

**Rules to put at the top:**
- Ask them to answer in full sentences that repeat the question (the interviewer is cut).
- When they summarise, ask "Take me to that moment. Where were you, who was there, what was said?"
- Ask for the number at every stage.
- Let silences run. The emotional pauses are the gold.
- Ask family questions late, when they're warmed up.
- Ask the cold-open questions last.

**Write 1–5 scene-based questions per beat,** prioritising every ❓. Always include:
- What their old job cost them and their family; the specific day it went wrong.
- "Did any part of you think, could I actually do this?"
- What skills from their old career they used straight away.
- The crossroads: "When was the van full? Why scale instead of keep working it?"
- "What's it like being responsible for other people's jobs, after what happened to you?"
- "Would you have done this on your own? Knowing what you know now, could you?"
- "What's a normal week like now compared with before?" "What does this mean for your family's future?"
- "If you hadn't made the jump, where would you be?"
- Cold open (last): "In two sentences, what's your story?" "What was the lowest point?" "What would you say to someone where you were?"
