## 3. Privacy notice (shown at /privacy on each brand's pages)

**Privacy notice**

Last updated: [date]

**Who we are**

[Brand legal name] ("[Brand]", "we"), company number [number], [registered address], is responsible for your details. Franchise Right Ltd runs this website and our franchise enquiries on our behalf.

To ask anything about your details, email [brand email].

**What we collect**

- What you tell us on the form: your name, email, phone number, postcode and your answers to the questions.
- What you do afterwards: which of our pages and documents you open, how much of each video you watch, and which of our emails you open and click.
- Calls: when calls are booked, whether they happened, a recording and written transcript of video calls, and our notes and our view after a call, including whether we think the franchise is a good fit for you.
- Where you came from: which advert brought you here, and the click code Meta adds to the advert's link.
- Technical details: your internet address and browser type.

Please do not tell us health details or other sensitive personal information. We do not need it.

**What we use it for**

- To send you the franchise prospectus and the other information you asked for.
- To contact you by email and phone about this franchise.
- To make what we send you relevant. For example, we may mention a document you have read or suggest a next step you have not taken.
- To judge whether the franchise and you look like a good fit. Your form answers are scored automatically, and we use the score to decide who to call first. If you do not meet a requirement we have set, you will see a short thank-you message and we will not contact you further. If you think that is wrong, email [brand email] and a person will review it.
- To record and analyse calls, including with AI software, so that we have an accurate record and can improve how we explain the franchise.
- To measure and improve our advertising (see "Meta" below).

We never use age or gender to decide who we speak to.

Franchise Right Ltd also uses anonymised, combined figures from many enquiries to improve its service. These cannot identify you.

**Our legal grounds**

- **Your consent**, given when you ticked the box on the form: contacting you by email and phone; recording and analysing calls; cookies and tracking that show us which pages you view, which videos you watch and which emails you open; sharing details with Meta.
- **Our legitimate interests** in finding suitable franchisees and running our recruitment properly: judging fit, and keeping records of our dealings with you.

You can withdraw your consent at any time (see "Your choices").

**Meta (Facebook and Instagram)**

We tell Meta when you sign up, book a call, have a call we judge a good fit, or join us (including the franchise fee). We send your email, phone number, name and postcode in a scrambled (hashed) form that Meta can only match to an account you already have, together with a reference number we give you, the advert's click code, your internet address and browser type. Meta uses this to show us which adverts work and to show our adverts to people more likely to be interested. Meta also handles this information under its own privacy policy.

**Cookies and similar storage**

- **Before you sign up:** none from us. The spam check on the form is run by Cloudflare, which sees your internet address and browser to tell people from automated programs.
- **After you sign up:** one cookie of our own, so the pages remember you and we can see what you read and watch. It lasts 180 days.
- **After you sign up, if you agreed on the form:** Meta's advertising cookies, which tell Meta which of our pages you look at, and a small note in your browser so your sign-up is only counted once.

You can clear or block cookies in your browser at any time.

**Who else handles your details**

Companies that provide our systems, under contract and only on our instructions:

- Franchise Right Ltd (UK): runs this website and our enquiry system.
- Amazon Web Services (Sweden): hosting, email and backups.
- Neon (London): the database.
- Cloudflare: the spam check on the form.
- Zoom: video calls and their recordings.
- Anthropic: AI analysis of call transcripts.
- Meta: advertising measurement, as described above.

Sweden is covered by the UK's rules allowing transfers to Europe. Cloudflare, Zoom, Anthropic and Meta may handle details in the United States, under the UK-US data bridge or standard contract terms approved for UK transfers. We never sell your details.

**How long we keep it**

- Your details and our record of contact with you: [2 years] after we last had contact, including if you did not meet our requirements.
- Call recordings and transcripts: [12 months] after the call.
- Backups: 35 days.
- If you unsubscribe, or your email address stops working, we keep the address on a "do not email" list so that we do not email you again.

If you become a franchisee, your details move into our franchisee records.

**Your choices**

- Every marketing email has an unsubscribe link, which works straight away. If you have booked a call, we will still send you the confirmation and reminders for that call.
- To stop phone calls, tell us on a call or by email.
- You have the right to see the details we hold about you, have them corrected or deleted, object to or limit how we use them, and receive a copy. Email [brand email] and we will reply within one month.

**Complaints**

Please tell us first so we can put it right. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office: ico.org.uk, 0303 123 1113.

## 4. Call recording

**In the booking confirmation and reminder emails (one line, already in franOS):**

> The call is recorded, so we have an accurate note of what was said.

**Said by the consultant at the start of every call:**

> "Just so you know, this call is being recorded so we have an accurate record. Is that OK?"

If the person says no, the consultant stops the recording and carries on.

## 5. Emails that react to what someone did

franOS's standard emails say "I saw you had a look at the prospectus" and "I saw you watched [video]". These are allowed, because the tick and the notice tell people we see what they read and watch. Limits for the copy (decision 7):

- Name the thing and offer help. Nothing more.
- Never mention times, devices, locations, or how many times something was opened.
- Never react to something they did before signing up.

franOS does not enforce these: anyone who can edit a brand's emails can break them.

## 6. The earnings note (foot of every page)

> Any earnings figures shown are projections based on the performance of existing franchisees. They are not a guarantee of what you will earn.

Rules that go with it: every figure traces to a source the franchisor has confirmed; a franchisee's own figures are used only with their written permission; say whether a figure is turnover or profit, and the period it covers.

## 7. Where this is weakest without a lawyer

1. **One required tick for everything.** The regulator's strict reading wants consent for cookies and marketing to be optional and separate from each other. Ours is one tick the form cannot be sent without (decision 109). This is the most likely point of challenge.
2. **Tracking under the tick.** Seeing which pages, videos and emails a named person opens counts as tracking under the cookie rules. The tick names it from 16 Nov 2026; leads who signed up before then agreed to the narrower wording.
3. **Who is responsible.** The notice names the brand as responsible and Franchise Right as working for it. That needs a signed data agreement with each client, which is not drafted yet. Franchise Right's own use of combined figures also needs to be in that agreement.
4. **Keeping periods** are promised in the notice but not yet enforced by franOS or in Zoom.
5. **AI analysis of calls** is named before it is built, so the notice is true from day one. A written risk assessment for it (a DPIA) is expected by the regulator and is not drafted yet.
6. **Phone calls.** The tick is what allows marketing calls to people on the Telephone Preference Service. Without the tick, they could not be called.
7. **People the form turns away** have only the brand's contact email as their route to a person, so that inbox has to be read.

8. **Franchise Right is not registered with the ICO** (Adam's decision). Most businesses that handle people's details have to pay the yearly fee, and the regulator can fine those that do not. Clients and their lawyers may ask for the registration number.
