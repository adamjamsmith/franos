## 1. The offer in one line
*Draft:* A way into the renovation business for someone who can run a company but has never been a builder: you sell and manage, a team does the building, on a system proven in the UK since 2017. £35,000 franchise fee.

## 2. Who it is for, and who it is not
From avatar card v0 (one interview; still to confirm):
1. Has run a business, or managed teams or budgets at a senior level.
2. Has the investment plus 6 to 12 months of living costs.
3. Wants to build a company with a team, not work on the tools.
4. Has a real connection to property: landlord, own renovation, interest in design.
5. Wants to start soon.

**Put off:** tradespeople who cannot run a business; first-time owners expecting hand-holding; people who will not spend on marketing; part-timers (open question for Paul: seven of the ten are part-time).

## 4. What it costs and what they get
- **Fee:** £35,000 (Refresh's data pack and sales Q&A). The public site still shows £20,000 and £45,000; confirm those are finished.
- **Total investment:** £35,000 to £75,000. Working capital on top: roughly £3,000 to £5,000 a month, before the owner's pay (to confirm).
- **Ongoing:** 6% royalty plus 1% national marketing, on money received. Local marketing at least £750 a month.
- **Funding:** start-up loan and bank routes are named by Refresh but not evidenced. Do not say "approved" for anything.
- **They get:** induction in New Zealand (about 7 days, paid by Refresh), the Control system, a franchise business manager, weekly huddle, quarterly reviews, a project-management partner, central admin.
- **Territory:** shared regions, one franchise per £50m of yearly renovation spend. The list of open territories has not arrived.
- **Their job:** generating their own leads. Head office does brand marketing only. Say this plainly.

## 5. The narrative
- **Core message (Adam, 14 Nov 2026):** if you really want to get into renovation, this is a way in that you could not have managed alone.
- **Where they are now:** senior, capable, done with building someone else's company. Drawn to property. Know they could sell a better renovation experience, but have no trade, no team and no system.
- **The turn:** a trigger. Redundancy, leaving a 9 to 5, or a bad renovation of their own ("I can sell the solution to this").
- **The belief to change:** "You need to be a builder to run a building company." The truth: builders were tried and did not work. It is a management business. It is also hard, hands-on work and not a business that runs itself; say so.
- **Whose story carries it:** Ali Irteza (sales director, no construction background, West London since 2022), recorded 4 Nov. Simon Kelliher (East of England since 2017, the largest) is not booked yet.

## 6. Objections and honest answers
*To develop with Mike, who has to say these on the call.*
- **"Will I be left on my own?"** Ali had a dedicated manager in 2022; he says newer franchisees get peer support and struggle. We need Paul's answer on what a new franchisee gets today.
- **"How long until my first job?"** The site says 1 to 2 months. Ali says month 5 to 6. Use the cautious figure and the money-to-live-on qualifier.
- **"What will I actually take home?"** No net figure exists in anything we hold. Open question for Paul.
- **"I've never run a building job."** The team prices and delivers; the owner sells and manages. Ali only got it right once he hired qualified project managers.
- **"Someone else can open next door."** Shared territories by design; franchisees share exhibition costs. Needs a plain answer from Paul.
